# Claims guide

Editorial guidance for a preclinical life-sciences company. Not legal advice; regulatory counsel should approve investor
offering materials, press releases with financial information, and any patient-facing copy.

## Why this matters

- **Investors:** statements in pitch decks and press releases can create liability if they overstate data,
  timelines or certainty. Forward-looking statements need a cautionary disclaimer and hedged language.
- **Regulators:** before approval, promotional claims about safety or efficacy of an investigational drug are
  not allowed. Describe what studies showed, in which model, without implying the drug works in people.
- **Credibility with KOLs and partners:** precise, qualified numbers read as rigorous; hype reads as naive.

## Rules of thumb

1. **Model + measure + duration + n** for every result. "In a mouse fibrosis model (n = …), 8 weeks of oral dosing
   restored 62% of healthy tissue elasticity."
2. **Preclinical framing.** "Preclinical", "in animal models", "ex vivo in donated human tissue". Never imply
   human efficacy; ex vivo human tissue data are not clinical data.
3. **Hedge the future.** "designed to", "aims to", "expected to", "plans to", "target". Timelines are targets.
4. **No absolutes.** Avoid "first", "only", "best", "all", "never", "proven", "guaranteed", "cure", "safe".
   If a "first/only" is true, it needs a source and a defined scope.
5. **Fair comparisons.** Same model, same endpoint, same duration. Give both numbers, not just the ratio.
6. **Competitors.** State facts about approvals and published data neutrally; no disparagement.
7. **Market and deal figures.** Name the source and year; label estimates as estimates.
8. **Images.** No before/after vision images that imply a clinical result; label simulations as illustrations.

## Disclaimer (investor and external decks)

Use the Forward-Looking Statements slide from the deck template (`add_disclaimer(prs)`), placed right after
the cover. Counsel should approve the final wording.
